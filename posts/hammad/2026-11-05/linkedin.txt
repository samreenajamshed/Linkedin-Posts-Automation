The stock was on the rack by 11 AM.
The system only saw it at 6 PM. Every order in between was cut short.

That gap has a name, and most warehouses measure it wrong.

Dock-to-stock time: how long received goods take to become available to pick and sell.

The rule:
Dock-to-stock time = hours from truck unloaded to stock pickable in the system
Average dock-to-stock = total dock-to-stock hours ÷ receipts

Worked example, illustrative numbers:
→ 08:00 truck unloaded, 40 pallets
→ 11:00 pallets put away
→ 18:00 goods receipt, GRN, posted and QA released

Floor view: 3 hours. Real dock-to-stock: 10 hours.
For 7 of those hours the stock was there, but order allocation could not see it.

The common mistake: stopping the clock at the rack. Customers feel the system clock, not the floor clock.

What to do Monday:
1. Pull unload time and GRN posting time for last week's receipts.
2. Split the gap: unload to put-away, put-away to posting, posting to release.
3. Post the GRN at put-away by scan, not at end of shift.
4. Set a target per inbound type, chilled and fast movers first.
