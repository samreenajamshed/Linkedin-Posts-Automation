6 turns a year sounds fine. Until you split it by SKU.
One healthy average can hide a year of dead stock.

Finance wants to know how fast the money sitting in stock comes back.

Inventory turns answers that.

Inventory turns: how many times a year your average stock is sold and replaced.

The rule:
Inventory turns = cost of goods sold ÷ average inventory at cost
Days of inventory = 365 ÷ turns

Worked example, illustrative numbers:
→ Cost of goods sold: 120 million a year
→ Average inventory at cost: 20 million
→ Turns = 120 ÷ 20 = 6
→ Days of inventory = 365 ÷ 6 = about 61 days

Two mistakes I see often:
❌ Sales value on top, cost at the bottom. It flatters the number.
❌ One month-end snapshot used as the average. Average the 12 month-ends.

And the big one: a site at 6 turns can hold fast movers at 20 and slow SKUs at 1.

Monday morning:
1. Calculate turns by SKU, not just by site.
2. List every SKU under 2 turns.
3. Decide for each one: cut orders, move it through a promotion, or clear it.
