Freight rates didn't change. The freight bill still crept up.
The trucks were leaving the gate with air in them.

It's a pattern I've seen more than once. Every urgent order gets its own truck. Every distributor wants the early slot. Nobody signs off a cost increase, yet cost per case drifts up month after month.

Teach: vehicle fill rate.
Plain English: how full a truck is when it leaves the gate.

Vehicle fill rate = cases loaded ÷ truck capacity in cases

Illustrative numbers:
→ Truck capacity 1,000 cases, average load 650. Fill rate: 65%.
→ 100 trips a month move 65,000 cases.
→ At 90% fill, the same cases need about 73 trips.
→ That's 27 trips paid for to move air.

Common mistake: watching the rate per trip and ignoring fill. A cheap trip at 60% full is an expensive case.

What to do Monday morning:
1. Report fill rate by route every day, next to OTD.
2. Set a minimum load rule, with one named person who approves exceptions.
3. Plan loads by route and drop sequence, not order by order.
4. Agree delivery windows with distributors so orders can be combined.

Fill and service pull against each other. The job is to see that trade-off, not hide it.
