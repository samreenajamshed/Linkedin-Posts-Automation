Zambia taught me to plan the bottle before the drink.
In beverages, a production plan without preforms is just a wish.

At Big Tree Beverages in Lusaka, my job included turning sales forecasts into production plans, then making sure the preforms and sleeves were there to run them.

The plastic plants needed our numbers early. Inbound shipments had their own lead times. So the real plan was not the filling schedule. It was the material plan behind it.

Supply Chain 101: net requirements.
The quantity you still need to order after counting what you have and what is already coming.

Net requirement = planned need minus stock on hand minus confirmed inbound plus safety buffer

Illustrative numbers, preforms for the next 2 weeks:
→ Plan: 600,000 bottles, so 600,000 preforms
→ On hand: 250,000
→ Confirmed inbound: 200,000
→ Safety buffer: 50,000
→ Net requirement: 200,000 to order now

Common mistake: counting stock that is blocked, damaged or already promised to another line.

Monday morning: take your top 5 SKUs, break each into its packaging, and check the net requirement against each supplier's lead time.
