Two SKUs. Same 90% forecast accuracy.
One ran out every month. The other never did.

The difference was bias, and most S&OP packs don't show it.

Forecast bias: whether your forecast misses in the same direction, again and again.

Bias % = total forecast minus total actual, then ÷ total actual

Illustrative numbers, 4 weeks, actual sales 1,000 cases a week:
→ SKU A: forecast 900 every week. Accuracy 90%. Bias minus 10%.
→ SKU B: forecast 1,100, 900, 1,100, 900. Accuracy 90%. Bias 0%.

SKU B's misses cancel out. Safety stock absorbs them.
SKU A is short 100 cases every single week. Safety stock drains, then the shelf empties.

Accuracy tells you how big the miss is.
Bias tells you which way it leans.

The common mistake: celebrating a good accuracy score while a steady lean goes unnoticed.

What to do Monday morning:
1. Put a bias column next to accuracy in your S&OP pack.
2. Flag any SKU that misses in the same direction 3 periods in a row.
3. Ask who adjusted that forecast last, and on what assumption.

Which do you report today: accuracy, bias, or both?
